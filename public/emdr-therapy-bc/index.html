When a memory feels less like the past and more like a live wire.

You may understand what happened intellectually and still feel your body react as if it is happening again. EMDR can help work with that gap—but it is not a shortcut or a one-size-fits-all fix.

Ask about EMDR

We do not start with the hardest memory.

Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing (EMDR) is a structured therapy approach for working with distressing memories and the beliefs, sensations, or responses connected to them. Whether it is a fit depends on what you are dealing with now, how stable life feels, and what support you have outside the session.

Preparation comes first. We will clarify your goals, build grounding strategies, and talk plainly about what the process involves. If EMDR makes sense, I will integrate it thoughtfully rather than treating it like a fast track through your history.

Stabilize before reprocessing

You need ways to stay connected to the present before we ask your nervous system to revisit something difficult.

Stay involved in every decision

You can pause, ask questions, or change direction. EMDR is something we do together, not something that is done to you.

Use the approach that fits the work

EMDR may sit alongside ACT, somatic, relational, and person-centred therapy rather than replacing everything else.
