Book a free 15-minute consultation.

An introduction by phone or video, with time to ask about OCD and anxiety counselling, fees and availability. All counselling sessions are online for adults in Vancouver and across BC.

Choose a consultation timeEmail a question

What would you like to know before beginning?

Perhaps you want to understand how ERP handles mental rituals, what ACT looks like in practice, or whether the online format would suit you. The consultation is a chance to ask those questions and hear how Unravel approaches care.

We can cover the support you are seeking, the practice’s scope, appointment availability and session fees. Limited reduced-fee places can be discussed if available. You do not need to give a detailed account of a distressing thought or experience to make an enquiry.

The free consultation lasts 15 minutes and is available by phone or video. It is an introduction rather than a full counselling session or formal diagnostic assessment.

Who can attend?

Unravel offers individual virtual counselling to adults aged 18 and over who are physically located in British Columbia during care. Vancouver clients attend online, as do clients elsewhere in BC. Meet Theekshitha Vadladi and review her education and completed training.

What happens after the consultation?

If working together seems appropriate and you choose to continue, the next step is to arrange an individual counselling appointment and review the intake information. Early sessions explore the pattern affecting your life, your circumstances and the goals that will guide the work.

Prefer to ask by email?

Email theekshitha@unravelcounselling.com with your name and practical question. For a care enquiry, it helps to say that you are in British Columbia and briefly name the kind of support you are seeking. Save highly sensitive clinical details for an appropriate care setting.

Privacy and urgent support

Email is not a crisis or emergency service and should not be used for urgent support. If you are in immediate danger or need urgent help, call 911 or contact a local crisis service. Read the website privacy notice for information about initial enquiries and privacy questions.
