GETTING TO KNOW EACH OTHER

Your free 20-minute consultation

We’ll use this phone or video call to talk about what you want help with and whether my practice is a good fit. I’ll ask for a brief sense of what has been happening, what you’re hoping counselling could change, and any previous therapy you want me to know about.

I’ll explain how I might work with you, answer your questions, and go over fees and appointment options. You can take time afterwards to decide whether you’d like to book a session.

THE FIRST APPOINTMENT

Your first counselling session

We’ll begin with the difficulties that brought you here and how they are affecting your day. I’ll ask about what you’ve tried, what has helped, and what you would like to be able to do more freely. We’ll also talk through confidentiality and the arrangements for working together online.

If OCD is part of what you’re experiencing, I’ll ask about checking, reassurance, avoidance and the rituals that can happen entirely in your head. This helps us understand what happens around a distressing thought and where counselling could help. We may need more than one appointment to build that picture. I’ll explain my initial understanding and discuss a starting point with you.

AN INDIVIDUAL PLAN

How we tailor the work

Your goals give the work a direction. That might mean getting through a morning without repeated checking, spending less time caught in worry, or returning to something you’ve been avoiding.

In anxiety counselling, we’ll use ACT to work on how you respond to worry and avoidance, with steps connected to what you want to do. For OCD, we’ll plan ERP around the situations and compulsions affecting you. ACT can help connect that practice with the life you want to take part in. We’ll discuss what an exercise involves, why I’m suggesting it, and how it fits your circumstances.

Culture and faith can change what needs to be understood before choosing a step. For someone whose faith matters deeply, that can include distinguishing a valued religious practice from a ritual driven by doubt. We’ll also consider privacy, responsibilities and the time available for practice.

FEEDBACK & ADJUSTMENTS

If counselling isn’t feeling helpful

I’ll ask how the sessions are landing and what is changing outside them. You can tell me when an explanation misses the point, an exercise feels confusing, or we’re spending time on something that matters less to you.

We’ll look at what needs adjusting: our understanding of the difficulty, the goals, the practice or the way I’m explaining it. In OCD work, that includes noticing whether a visible ritual has been replaced by mental checking. Progress might show up as less time spent on rituals or more participation in daily life, even while some anxiety remains.

If your needs would be better met by a different clinician or service, I’ll discuss that with you and help you consider the options.

ABOUT UNRAVEL

What the name Unravel means.

To unravel something is to understand how its threads became tangled. In counselling, that can mean noticing a cycle of doubt and rituals, a familiar response to worry, or an expectation that has become difficult to carry.

Change can involve loosening those patterns while keeping the commitments that matter. The direction is personal: being more present with someone you love, completing a task without prolonged checking, or making a decision without waiting for complete confidence.
