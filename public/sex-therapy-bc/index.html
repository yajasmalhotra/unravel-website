You should not have to perform “normal” in therapy too.

Desire, intimacy, and sex do not happen in a vacuum. Stress, shame, culture, health, identity, relationship history, and the pressure to be effortless can all follow you into the bedroom.

Book a consultation

There is no gold standard for how intimacy is supposed to look.

Maybe desire has changed and neither of you knows how to talk about it without someone feeling rejected. Maybe sex brings anxiety, disconnection, pain, shame, or the sense that you are playing a role. Or perhaps you have questions you have never been able to say out loud.

More sex is not automatically the goal. Neither is forcing every couple into the same version of closeness or pretending culture and family messages can simply be ignored. We will look directly at the patterns shaping your experience and decide what a more honest, connected relationship with intimacy means for you.

Say the thing that is hard to name

We can talk about desire, self-worth, identity, shame, intimacy, and communication without expecting you to arrive with perfect language.

Question the script you inherited

Family, faith, gender expectations, culture, and past relationships can all shape what you believe you are allowed to want.

Set the pace yourself

You will not be pressured to disclose more than feels useful. Consent applies to the therapy conversation too.
