A PLACE TO BEGIN

More choice in how you respond.

Acceptance and Commitment Therapy, pronounced as the word “act”, is a behavioural therapy focused on psychological flexibility. In plain language, that means noticing the present situation and responding in ways that serve your values, even when difficult thoughts or feelings are present. The Association for Contextual Behavioral Science explains ACT and its purpose.

At Unravel, ACT informs work with anxiety and the wider framework for OCD counselling. It is used with adults aged 18 and over through virtual sessions in Vancouver and across British Columbia. Your circumstances give the work its meaning: the decisions, responsibilities and relationships you are trying to navigate.

THE CORE PROCESSES

Six connected skills, explained simply.

ACT brings together six processes. They are connected ways of developing flexibility, rather than stages to pass or standards to perform perfectly. ACBS describes the six core processes.

  • Acceptance: willingness to experience a difficult feeling when doing so supports a meaningful action.
  • Defusion: noticing a thought as an event in your mind rather than automatically following it as a rule.
  • Present-moment awareness: directing attention to what is happening now, with flexibility to return when it wanders.
  • Perspective: recognising that a difficult thought, feeling or label is part of your experience rather than the whole of who you are.
  • Values: identifying the qualities you want to bring to the way you live and relate to others.
  • Committed action: taking useful steps in those directions and adjusting in response to what you learn.

IN SESSIONS

What ACT can look like in a session.

A session may start with a specific moment: an avoided decision, a familiar worry or an interaction that did not go as you wanted. The focus is the relationship between what showed up internally, how you responded and what that response made possible or difficult.

The work can include conversation, an experiential exercise, a metaphor or a short practice in noticing thoughts and feelings. The purpose of an exercise should be clear. Language and examples need to fit the individual, rather than asking everyone to connect with the same metaphor.

For example, a person who values friendship might repeatedly decline invitations while waiting to feel entirely confident. ACT would explore the worry and the choice in relation to that value. The next step would be chosen in light of their needs, circumstances and what connection means to them.

VALUES & CONTEXT

Values belong in the context of your life.

Values are qualities you want to express in how you live, rather than a list of outcomes you must achieve. They might concern care, honesty, connection, learning or another direction that has meaning for you. Your counsellor does not choose those priorities for you.

At Unravel, values work includes culture, family, faith, identity and responsibilities. It does not assume that independence is everyone’s central goal, or that connection with family prevents change. Commitments can be examined with respect for their meaning and their cost.

Acceptance concerns your relationship with internal experiences. It does not require approving every thought or accepting a harmful circumstance. Practical changes, boundaries and sources of support can still be part of the work. The ACBS public introduction describes ACT’s emphasis on values and flexible behaviour.

ACT WITHIN OCD CARE

How ACT relates to OCD-specific ERP.

For OCD, Exposure and Response Prevention addresses the compulsive response within the cycle. ACT can support willingness, attention and the direction of the work. These are different roles, and the distinction matters when choosing OCD care.

The IOCDF treatment guide identifies ERP as a first-line treatment and ACT as an adjunctive approach. At Unravel, the role of each approach is explained within a care plan that remains specific to the OCD cycle and your goals.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Questions about the approach.

Does acceptance mean agreeing with a thought?

No. In ACT, acceptance concerns willingness to experience what happens internally. It does not require believing every thought, following every urge or approving a harmful situation.

Will ACT stop anxious thoughts?

ACT does not promise that difficult thoughts will disappear. It focuses on developing a more flexible relationship with them and choosing responses linked to your values.

Do I need to know how to meditate?

No meditation background is required. Mindful attention is introduced in relation to the work, and exercises can be explained in ordinary language.

Is ACT counselling offered online across BC?

Yes. Unravel offers individual virtual counselling to adults aged 18 and over in Vancouver and throughout British Columbia. Clients must be physically in BC during sessions.
